Concordance Validation

Does the LLM judge in our SAE auto-interpretability pipeline measure what we claim? Nine direction sources, two judges, four metrics, six disjoint |r| intervals — all on held-out notes.

The argumentSeven findings, in the order they build

Each depends on the one before it. Together they say: the pooled metric fails, we know why, and the metric that survives holds under a harder test.

  1. YES+PARTIAL is not a valid measure — random directions beat the SAE on it. At |r| 0.1–0.3, random scores 79% against the SAE's 58% (p = 6.4×10−5). A metric that noise wins is not measuring signal.
  2. The failure is localised to PARTIAL, not to the judge. exact-YES over the same features: SAE 0%, random 2%, p = 0.35. Same judge, same prompt, same features. Only the pooling differs.
  3. PARTIAL is a real category, not an artifact of offering it. Forced to choose, only 6–16% of PARTIALs become YES; the rest become NO. The judge was correctly declining to call clinically adjacent concepts a match.
  4. Susceptibility to the r-anchor is judge-specific, not a property of the prompt. Removing the stated correlation leaves Sonnet unchanged (83–88% of YES verdicts retained) while DeepSeek loses two-thirds (35–45% retained), on identical features. The judge behind the published numbers is the robust one. A single-judge concordance report without this check can hide a large artifact.
  5. Hard negatives are what make the SAE result significant. Same-chapter distractors: JumpReLU +38.2pp (p = 0.003) versus +15.8pp and ns under cross-chapter. The easy slate let random directions win on organ-system plausibility alone.
  6. Label-supervised directions score below random. Diff-in-means 24%, probe LR 17%, random 38% at |r| 0.3–0.5 — despite both seeing the ICD labels and reaching |r| 0.70. Prediction and description come apart. This is what the SAE contributes.
  7. Two SAEs replicate; the general-purpose SAE does not compete. JumpReLU and ReLU+L1 agree within 3pp everywhere. GemmaScope has 13 features above r>0.4 against 276. Domain training, not architecture, is what matters.

For discussionA proposed results structure

One way to sequence this. The ordering follows the findings above — each section earns the next. Open questions are flagged.

  1. The pooled metric fails a floor test Table: exact-YES vs YES+PARTIAL for SAE and random across |r|. One p-value carries it (6.4×10−5). Open: do we lead with this or with the anchoring result?
  2. Why it fails — PARTIAL captures clinical adjacency The binary experiment. Establishes PARTIAL is genuine, so the fix is reporting, not re-prompting.
  3. A second reporting artifact — the correlation anchor Arm 0 vs Arm 1. Currently DeepSeek-only; needs Sonnet before it can be a headline.
  4. What survives: hit@1 under hard negatives Both conditions, both judges. Argues the metric choice with data rather than assertion.
  5. Is an SAE necessary? Supervised and unsupervised sources at matched |r|. Strongest single row in the paper.
  6. Robustness Two architectures replicate; two judges agree on exact-YES and hit@1 but not on YES+PARTIAL.
  7. Limitations 16 random directions above |r| 0.3 (single seed). GemmaScope's hard-negative cell is ns at n=9. No human validation — κ measures model–model agreement only.
